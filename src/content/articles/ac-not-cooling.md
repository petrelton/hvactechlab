If your air conditioner is running but your home isn't getting cool, there are several possible causes. Some are simple problems you can check yourself, while others require an HVAC technician.

## Quick things to check

Before calling a technician, check these common issues:

- Make sure the thermostat is set to **Cool** and the temperature is set below the current room temperature.
- Check that the indoor and outdoor units are receiving power.
- Check whether the circuit breaker for the air conditioner has tripped.
- Make sure the air filter isn't excessively dirty.
- Check that supply vents are open and not blocked by furniture.
- Make sure the outdoor condenser isn't obstructed by leaves, grass or other debris.

If these checks don't solve the problem, the air conditioner may have a mechanical or refrigerant-related fault.

## Common reasons an air conditioner isn't cooling

### 1. Dirty air filter

A severely clogged air filter restricts airflow through the system. This can reduce cooling performance and, in some cases, cause the evaporator coil to become too cold and freeze.

Check the filter and replace it if it is dirty. How frequently filters need to be replaced depends on the filter type, system and conditions in the home.

### 2. Thermostat problems

The thermostat controls when the air conditioner operates. Incorrect settings, a faulty thermostat or a temperature sensor problem can prevent the system from cooling properly.

Make sure the thermostat is set to **Cool**, the fan is set appropriately, and the temperature setting is below the current indoor temperature.

### 3. Dirty outdoor condenser

The outdoor unit needs to release heat from your home. If the condenser coil is heavily covered with dirt or debris, heat transfer can be reduced and the system may struggle to cool effectively.

Keep vegetation and other obstructions away from the outdoor unit.

### 4. Frozen evaporator coil

If the indoor evaporator coil freezes, the air conditioner may stop cooling effectively even though the system continues to run.

Restricted airflow, a dirty filter, a refrigerant problem or other faults can contribute to coil icing.

If you can see ice on the indoor unit or refrigerant lines, **turn the air conditioner off and allow the ice to thaw** rather than continuing to run the system.

### 5. Low refrigerant

Air conditioners are designed to operate with a specific amount of refrigerant. A system that is low on refrigerant may not cool properly.

Refrigerant doesn't normally get "used up." If the refrigerant level is low, there may be a leak that needs to be located and repaired.

Refrigerant-related work should be performed by a properly qualified HVAC professional.

### 6. Faulty compressor

The compressor is one of the most important components of a central air-conditioning system. It circulates refrigerant between the indoor and outdoor components.

A failing compressor can cause poor cooling or prevent the system from cooling altogether. Compressor problems generally require professional diagnosis.

## When should you call an HVAC technician?

You can safely perform simple checks such as examining the thermostat, checking the circuit breaker and replacing a dirty air filter.

You should consider calling an HVAC technician if:

- The air conditioner still isn't cooling after basic checks.
- The system repeatedly trips the circuit breaker.
- You see ice on the indoor unit or refrigerant lines.
- The outdoor unit isn't operating normally.
- You suspect a refrigerant leak.
- The system makes unusual noises or smells.
- The air conditioner starts and stops repeatedly.

## How much does AC repair cost?

The cost of repairing an air conditioner depends heavily on the problem. A simple component or electrical repair may cost considerably less than replacing a compressor or repairing a refrigerant leak.

For a better estimate, see our guide to **[air conditioner repair costs](/ac-repair-cost/)**.

## Repair or replace your air conditioner?

If an air conditioner is relatively new and has a straightforward fault, repairing it will often make more sense than replacing the entire system.

Older systems with repeated breakdowns, declining efficiency or expensive component failures may make replacement worth considering.

The right decision depends on the age and condition of the system, its efficiency, repair history and the expected cost of continued operation.

## Frequently asked questions

### Why is my AC running but not cooling?

Common causes include a dirty air filter, restricted airflow, a dirty outdoor condenser, a frozen evaporator coil, low refrigerant or a mechanical fault.

### Why is my AC blowing warm air?

Warm air can result from incorrect thermostat settings, restricted airflow, a frozen coil, refrigerant problems or faults with the outdoor unit or compressor.

### Can a dirty air filter cause an AC to stop cooling?

Yes. A severely restricted filter can reduce airflow enough to cause cooling problems and may contribute to an evaporator coil freezing.

### Should I turn off my AC if it is frozen?

Yes. If you see significant ice on the indoor unit or refrigerant lines, turn the system off and allow it to thaw. If the problem returns, have the system professionally diagnosed.

### How long should an air conditioner take to cool a house?

There isn't a single normal cooling time. It depends on the size and efficiency of the system, outdoor temperature, insulation, sunlight, indoor heat load and other factors. A properly operating system should nevertheless produce a noticeable temperature difference between the return and supply air.

## Bottom line

If your air conditioner is running but not cooling, start with the simple checks: thermostat settings, power, air filter, airflow and the outdoor unit.

If those don't solve the problem, the cause may involve refrigerant, electrical components or the compressor. In those cases, professional diagnosis is usually the safest approach.
